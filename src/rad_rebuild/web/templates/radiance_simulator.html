Metric Guide

          How to read the lighting metrics
        

        
      

      

        

          

            
Three questions these numbers answer

            

              Every metric in this panel is trying to answer one of three practical questions:
              how bright the canopy is, how evenly that light is distributed, and how much useful light remains
              once hotspots or power limits are taken seriously.
            

          

          

            

              Brightness
              How much light reaches the canopy?
              
Mean PPFD and photon totals tell you the overall delivery level.

            

            

              Uniformity
              How even is that light?
              
Ratios, CV, and DOU show whether the room is smooth or hotspot-heavy.

            

            

              Usability
              How much survives real limits?
              
Optional cap metrics estimate what is still useful after peak-capping or dimming pressure.

            

          

        


        

          

            
Core field stats

            
These describe the raw PPFD field on the canopy plane before any optional peak-capping logic is applied.

          

          

            

              
mean

              
The canopy-wide average PPFD. This is the single best quick read on overall intensity.

              Think of it as the room’s average light level.
            

            

              
min / max

              
The darkest and brightest sampled points on the canopy.

              They reveal the worst corner and worst hotspot.
            

            

              
p05 / p50 / p95

              
Percentiles that show the lower tail, midpoint, and upper tail of the PPFD field.

              They are more stable than just staring at the single darkest or brightest point.
            

          

        


        

          

            
Uniformity ratios

            
These numbers describe shape and smoothness, not just raw brightness.

          

          

            

              
peak/mean

              
How much brighter the hotspot is than the average canopy level.

              Closer to 1.0 usually means fewer hotspots.
            

            

              
min/mean

              
How the darkest point compares with the average canopy level.

              Higher is better because weak corners are less severe.
            

            

              
min/max

              
The darkest point divided by the brightest point.

              It is a blunt but intuitive “worst-case uniformity” ratio.
            

            

              
mean/peak

              
The average level compared with the hotspot.

              Higher values mean less of your system is “wasted” in peaks.
            

          

        


        

          

            
Photon delivery

            
These convert field statistics into total canopy photon flow and capture.

          

          

            

              
ppf_out

              
Total photosynthetic photon flow arriving on the sampled canopy area.

              This is output delivered to plants, not just emitted by fixtures.
            

            

              
ppf_emitted

              
Total photons the luminaires are assumed to emit into the room.

              It is the source-side total before losses in the room.
            

            

              
capture_frac

              
The fraction of emitted photons that end up on the sampled canopy area.

              Higher capture means less light is lost to walls, aisles, and overshoot.
            

          

        


        

          

            
Electrical metrics

            
These relate useful photon delivery back to input power.

          

          

            

              
watts_in

              
Total electrical input power assumed for the run.

              This is the electrical cost side of the comparison.
            

            

              
watts_at_cap

              
Estimated electrical input after global dimming needed to respect a PPFD cap.

              Only appears when peak capping is enabled for a dimmable system.
            

            

              
deuc_elec

              
Delivered efficacy under cap: useful canopy photons divided by electrical input.

              This is a practical “usable umol per joule” metric.
            

          

        


        

          

            
Peak-cap metrics

            

              When peak capping is enabled, these answer a real-world question: if the brightest point cannot exceed a ceiling,
              how much useful average light is left after you globally scale the system down?
            

          

          

            

              
cap / setpoint_ppfd

              
The PPFD ceiling being enforced for the analysis.

              Think of it as the maximum acceptable hotspot level.
            

            

              
cap_scale

              
The global dimming factor required so the brightest point no longer exceeds the cap.

              If this is far below 1.0, the room had major hotspot pressure.
            

            

              
dim_penalty

              
How much of the original output had to be given up to respect that cap.

              Higher penalty means more light was trapped in hotspots.
            

            

              
mean_at_cap / min_at_cap / p05_at_cap

              
The average, darkest point, and 5th percentile after applying the cap-scale.

              These show what the room actually looks like after the hotspot problem is corrected.
            

            

              
utilization_at_cap

              
The achieved average PPFD after capping divided by the cap itself.

              Higher means the design keeps more useful average light after hotspots are tamed.
            

            

              
ppf_at_cap

              
Total canopy photon delivery remaining after peak-capping.

              It translates hotspot penalties into lost usable photons.
            

            

              
cov±5%mean

              
The share of canopy points that land within plus or minus 5% of the achieved post-cap mean.

              Higher values mean a smoother, more uniform field.
            

            

              
score_hmean_5

              
A balanced score combining post-cap utilization and ±5% coverage around the mean.

              It rewards systems that are both usable and even after caps are enforced.
            

          

        


        

          

            
Legacy uniformity metrics

            
These are familiar from lighting analysis, but they do not directly capture hotspot-capping behavior.

          

          

            

              
std

              
The standard deviation of PPFD values across the canopy.

              Higher standard deviation means the room is less even.
            

            

              
cv_percent

              
Coefficient of variation: standard deviation divided by the mean.

              Lower CV is generally better for uniformity.
            

            

              
dou_percent

              
Degree of uniformity, derived from variation about the mean.

              Higher DOU indicates a smoother canopy field.
            

          

        

      

    

  


  

    

      

        

          
Project Context

          About Horticulture Lighting Simulator
          

            A Radiance-based horticultural lighting simulation and 3D visualization engine.
          

        

        
      

      

        

          
What this is

          

            This is a browser-accessible lighting simulator for comparing horticultural lighting layouts across
            Proposed LED, Conventional LED, and 1000W HPS systems. Public local playback uses precomputed Radiance
            results for speed and reproducibility.
          

        


        

          
Engineering highlights

          
    
            
  • Radiance-based optical simulation workflow.
    • 
            
  • Precomputed public playback architecture.
    • 
            
  • Proposed LED layout generation for square and rectangular spaces.
    • 
            
  • Multi-system comparison: Proposed LED, Conventional LED, and 1000W HPS.
    • 
            
  • Interactive Viridis PPFD heatmap with raw-value hover inspection.
    • 
            
  • High-performance Three.js/WebGL 3D assembly viewer.
    • 
            
  • Optimized CAD asset pipeline: build123d to STEP to GLB to high, medium, and proxy LODs.
    • 
            
  • Lightweight polished GLB assets with strong browser performance.
    • 
            
  • Production deployment is precomputed-only; live Docker/local Radiance modes are for trusted local clones.
    • 
          

        


        

          

            
Research and IP context

            
Created by Austin Rouse.

            

              The Proposed LED System relates to U.S. Patent No. 10,687,478,
              
                Optimized LED Lighting Array for Horticultural Applications.
            

            

              The simulation workflow supports a solo-author horticultural lighting manuscript currently under peer
              review in Lighting Research & Technology.
